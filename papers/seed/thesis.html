Digital Lutherie and Expression for the Electronic Artist: A 2027 Retrospective and Prospectus

Status: in development. A practice-based master's thesis, written in the first person because the practitioner is the site of the inquiry.

What it binds

Three pieces of work that only mean something together:

  • DemiurgeOS — the operating layer. What it takes to make a Raspberry Pi behave like an instrument instead of a computer.
  • Performance System of a Modern Digital Luthier — the instrument. What gets built on top of that layer, and what it means to walk onstage with nothing bought.
  • Workflow of a Modern Digital Luthier — the methodology. The claim that the first two are instances of something general.

The argument

Each paper alone is a system description. Together they are evidence for something larger: that the traditional sequence of digital instrument-making — specify, then program, then fabricate, then perform — has collapsed into a single iterative act that one practitioner can hold entirely in their head.

The thesis is the further-meta position on that. Not here is an instrument, and not here is how instruments get made, but what it means that the distance between wanting an instrument and having one has become this short, and what that does to the instruments themselves.

Scope

The argument runs on those three. BOUBA and NAM × Csound are the same season's work and can be cited as artifacts, but they aren't load-bearing here — BOUBA's user study in particular is evaluated by a methodology foreign to everything else, and ships on its own timeline as its own paper.

2. Why the three compound

Demiurge exists because the instrument needed it. The instrument is one of the two case studies that made the methodology arguable. The methodology is what the other two mean when read together — which is not a thing either of them can say alone.

Each paper alone is a system description. The thesis is the argument they add up to.
