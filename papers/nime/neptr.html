NEPTR Phase 4 (DEMIURGE): A Laptop-Free System for Performance and Improvisation

Emory Smith

EMGOR / California Institute of the Arts

Draft — NIME 2027 target

Abstract

(Working abstract — formal abstract TBD.) NEPTR is the brain at the center of the performance system, linking together audio interfaces, DIY controllers and hyper-instruments with internal DSP and GUI. It replaces the laptop, synthesizer and pedalboard onstage. Dense sensors and controls onboard with exposed USB ports (×3), Ethernet port and micro-HDMI port; performance happens using only DIY controllers and DIY hyper-instruments. Included in the NEPTR performance system: hyper-trumpet, hyper-guitar, hemi speaker(s), WE remote, DIY audio interface. The plan is to perform with it at NIME and ICMC this year, and hopefully present it as well.

Keywords: live performance, Csound, Raspberry Pi, hyper-instruments, DIY controllers, guitar effects

1. Introduction

NEPTR has moved through four phases; Phase 4 — codenamed DEMIURGE, after the Pi that runs it — is the instrument on stage right now. It is a guitar effects processor and performance brain for surf rock and experimental performance, feeding a real amplifier: everything is voiced as pedal or preamp, never cabinet simulation, because a real amp is doing that job. The laptop, the synthesizer, and the pedalboard are gone; what remains onstage is the Pi, a Teensy control surface, a touchscreen, and the DIY hyper-instruments that feed it.

2. System

2.1 Runtime

Csound runs on a Raspberry Pi 5 over raw ALSA — no PipeWire, no JACK — at 48 kHz with a 128-sample period, for roughly 7 ms round trip: guitar → interface → Csound → interface → amplifier. One .csd entry point #includes ~36 effect files, a globals file, the signal chain, and a MIDI handler; bypassed effects are turnoff2'd and cost zero CPU. A systemd service boots the whole instrument from a single live.conf, on the DEMIURGE_OS platform.

2.2 Control surface

A Teensy with seven encoders and an expression pedal drives everything. Physical CCs are remapped to logical roles: two encoders page through 19 menus (PRESETS, VOLUME, SPECTRAL, PITCH, FILTERING, MOD, DRIVE, PREAMP, GRANULAR, DIGIVERB, DELAY, BASS, SYNTH, AIR, LOOPER, CHASE BLISS, GLITCH, NAM, IR VERB), five toggleable effect slots per menu, and every rotation drives the last-toggled effect's parameters. The expression pedal follows a sticky last-toggled-wins rule. A pygame touchscreen UI is mirrored over OSC, with a consistency test suite that fails the build if the UI tables, the MIDI handler, and the reference documentation ever disagree.

2.3 The effect roster

Around 70 toggleable effects, every one hand-written Csound. Highlights: five circuit-modeled drive pedals (Klon/TS morph, Marshall preamp, SLO⊕5150 dual high-gain, RAT×Metal Zone, Big Muff) through shared 4×-oversampled clippers; ports of three Chase Bliss pedals, including a 175-second echo collector and a granular modifier of the looper's own loops; Neural Amp Modeler at the end of the chain via the custom NAMProcess opcode; a 5-channel looper with a drum-sample master-grid mode and a clocked GLITCH menu placed post-looper so it mangles loops too; spectral bin crushers and codec-degradation processors; pitch-tracked synth voices; convolution IR reverb; and CHAIN FB, which feeds the entire chain back into itself, bounded by construction.

2.4 The performance system

Around the brain sit the DIY satellites: hyper-trumpet and hyper-guitar, hemispherical speakers (Quadro Punch Packer), the We-Remote BLE controller, the 4-i-Gor DIY audio interface, and the oFOOTS foot controller — all documented as hardware planets on this site.

3. Current Status

Phase 4 is the current gigging instrument: deployed at ~7 ms round trip with the 19-menu surface, presets with per-preset output trim, and the NAM stage live. The looper menu is in active development, and the authoritative internal reference document is kept current as the system changes [2]. Latency work continues toward a 64-sample period (~4 ms) pending a worst-preset soak test.

4. Future Work

Finish the looper; complete the hyper-instrument set; and take the system to the stage at NIME and ICMC — performance first, presentation second. Possible collaboration with Dr. Boulanger and Ajay.
