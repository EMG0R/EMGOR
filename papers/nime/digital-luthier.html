Workflow of a Modern Digital Luthier: Unifying Embedded Audio and Graphics Computing, Digital Fabrication, and Purposeful Interaction Design

Emory Smith

EMGOR / California Institute of the Arts

Draft — NIME 2027 target

Abstract

The parallel maturation of single-board computers, microcontrollers, audio-visual programming languages, fabrication techniques, and access to information has created an environment where form emerges as a coherent system rather than in sequential layers. This convergence enables individuals to rapidly develop self-contained interfaces where gesture, sound, graphics, and physical structure are implemented as direct extensions of artistic intention. This paper presents a few case studies demonstrating this methodology. These examples illustrate how story-driven design across integrated domains produces coherent interactive identity and indicate where this practice is heading as these tools continue to mature.

Instrument Case Studies (working additions)

Candidate case studies from the EMGOR hardware planets, each an instance of the luthier workflow — embedded compute, fabricated body, and interaction design conceived as one system:

  • Quadro Punch Packer — 10" printed hemisphere speaker — 4 coaxials at the cube-vertex angle plus a sub.
  • We-Remote — open-source Wii remote — BLE gamepad, keyboard, and MIDI CC in one shell.
  • Pocket OpGorator — Pocket-Operator-format Daisy sampler/synth with a velocity NeoTrellis grid.
  • 4-i-Gor — Teensy 4.0 quad-in / quad-out USB audio interface with MIDI.
  • THE WATCH — forearm AMOLED smartwatch fused with an RF multitool and a sensor army.
  • LITE — Teensy 4.1 light instrument — 20-voice generative synth in a printed bowl.
  • oFOOTS — ten-encoder foot controller — 8 pages, 64 CCs, one expression pedal.
