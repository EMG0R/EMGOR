BOUBA: An Intuitive System for Accessing the Benefits of Music Therapy

Emory Smith

EMGOR / California Institute of the Arts

Draft — TEI / DIS target

Abstract

BOUBA is a system providing tactile interaction with therapeutic generative synthesis. Designed for the non-musician seeking a psychologically beneficial relationship with sound, BOUBA targets four pillars of human experience: sleep, mood, productivity, and autonomic regulation. At reproduction price, its physical controls and self-contained state foster an intimate and unique relationship with the user, grounded in empirical research linking ambient consonance, rhythmic entrainment, and generative soundscapes to measurable physiological and psychological benefit.

Keywords: music therapy, generative synthesis, tangible interfaces, Teensy, quadraphonic, wellbeing

1. Introduction

Most therapeutic-sound products are apps: another screen, another subscription, another stream. BOUBA takes the opposite position — a small, self-contained physical object whose controls are all onboard, whose state lives nowhere but inside it, and whose sound is generated rather than played back. The bet is that physicality and self-containment are not packaging details but part of the therapeutic mechanism: an intimate, single-purpose object invites a different relationship with sound than a phone does.

2. The Built Object

BOUBA is programmed with the Teensy Audio Library, centered around a number of generative compositions that are self-sufficient but interactive. It runs with no external connections besides power. Onboard controls select or interpolate between presets, manually trigger notes, and adjust volume, probability, or pitch.

Four speakers deliver quadraphonic output from two PCM5102 DACs amplified by four LM386 amplifiers. The system is powered from a standard 9 V jack which is stepped down for different components, allowing for use with common cables or batteries. It costs roughly \$80 USD to reproduce. The 3D-printed enclosure positions the speakers for quadraphonic imaging while centralizing controls for single-handed operation.

Evaluated against O'Modhrain's framework: playability is lower by design — a minimal control surface is the point — learnability is high, sonic range is intentionally constrained to consonant ambient territory, and robustness has been high over two months of consistent use.

2.1 The research baseline

Each preset operationalizes a published finding. For sleep, white noise improving sleep in coronary care patients and sine-based music reducing sleep latency by 38% with increased delta power. For mood, natural soundscapes facilitating mood recovery, and consonant harmonic relationships engaging paralimbic reward centers while dissonant stimuli activate amygdala regions tied to negative valence. For productivity, 45 dB white noise raising attention and creativity via stochastic resonance, and rhythmic entrainment at 120–140 BPM improving work output. For autonomic regulation, salivary cortisol halved post-stressor with ambient music, and a meta-analytic effect of d = 0.380 for physiological stress reduction across music interventions.

BOUBA does not attempt to replicate the clinical conditions of any cited study, and makes no claim of clinical efficacy. The relationship between these findings and BOUBA's synthesis parameters is one of informed design.

2.2 Second generation and the web version

A second hardware prototype is in development, distinct from the original and designed for the two things the first traded away: accessibility of reproduction, and sound quality. Alongside it, a free web version runs the same generative system in a browser, so the object can be used without being built.

The pair makes possible a comparison the object alone could not support: how people relate to a physical sound object versus the identical generative system on a screen.

3. Current Status

Built and in use. The first BOUBA has been in consistent use for months and appears as one of two case studies in Workflow of a Modern Digital Luthier. The second-generation prototype and the web version are the active work.

4. Evaluation Plan

The contribution offered is the interface and what people do with it, not a health outcome — a tangible-interface and research-through-design paper rather than a clinical one. Target venues: TEI or DIS, with Audio Mostly as the faster route.

Evaluation is qualitative: semi-structured interviews with a normal-for-the-field sample, thematic analysis, a standardized interface-quality instrument such as AttrakDiff or UEQ, and usage telemetry from the web build — which presets people return to, how long sessions run, what time of day they happen. The physical-versus-browser comparison is the central question.

Any study involving participants goes through institutional review before a single response is collected.
