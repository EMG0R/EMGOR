NeuralGrid: A Physical Interface for Live Sequencing and Improvisation

Emory Smith · Ajay Kapur · Jake Cheng

California Institute of the Arts

Draft — NIME target · mid-build

Abstract

NeuralGrid is a sensor-dense physical controller whose models speak a symbolic vocabulary before they speak sound. Pads, ribbons, lidar, and contact microphones are smoothed and interpolated into a coherent output format defined by the dimensional space the sensors represent. A first model interprets gesture through a symbolic vocabulary borrowed from tabla — where bols are spoken strokes, theka holds a cycle, and tihai and kaida act as geometric transforms over stroke sequences — so phrases can be played, combined, varied, and spoken aloud. A second model translates that symbolic stream onto any synthesis engine through seven perceptual axes. The translator is deaf by design: it never hears what it renders, which keeps model latency structurally outside the audio path.
