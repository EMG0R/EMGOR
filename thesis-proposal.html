01 · THE QUESTION

What changes when the luthier and the performer are the same person?

A retrospective names a territory. What decision does the question force?

How does holding the whole instrument stack — operating system, hardware, synthesis, enclosure — in one practitioner’s hands change what an electronic artist can express, compared with the laptop-and-gear ecosystem it replaces, within the constraint of no institutional resources?

It forces me to build everything, not most things. If any layer is bought instead of made, the comparison collapses.

Ten years ago a self-contained instrument cost an institution or a compromise. That gap has closed, and the field hasn’t fully noticed what it does to expression. Retrospective: what I’ve built. Prospectus: what I’m arguing.

02 · WHAT I’M MAKING

A performance where nothing onstage was bought that could have been built

What does a person in the room actually get?

The outcome — a laptop-free set at ICMC 2027 on the NEPTR performance system: Csound on my own OS, a Teensy control surface, hyper-guitar and hyper-trumpet, a printed hemispherical speaker, a second Pi rendering visuals over a cable protocol I wrote, NeuralGrid on the table. The thesis explains it; the performance is it.

The stack, already running — DemiurgeOS (Pi 5, PipeWire, a Rust supervisor) · Csound, 63 effect modules · Teensy · 3D-printed enclosures · OMNIPLEX transport · the NAMProcess opcode.

The method — each layer built with the layer below, as one act, with AI as a working collaborator at every step. No AI chapter; it’s in every chapter, the way it’s in every day.

05 · CONSTRAINT, SCOPE, NEXT

Only the instruments I already have. Only my own practice as data.

Can the claim survive if one feature disappears?

Drop any satellite and NEPTR still performs. Drop NEPTR and the claim is gone. That’s how I know the core. No new instruments get started; the data is my own practice, and the case that it extends to others is argued, not measured. No study, no IRB, no borrowed authority.
